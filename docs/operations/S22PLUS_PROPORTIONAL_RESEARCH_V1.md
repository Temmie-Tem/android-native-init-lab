# S22+ proportional research V1

Status: **REVIEW_GATED_CAPABILITY**. This common-incorporated policy applies
only to `SM-S906N/g0q/S906NKSS7FYG8`. Its reviewed implementation is
`s22plus_native_research_scope_v1.py`, the shared native-baseline owner and the
fixed `s22plus_native_reobservation_v1.py` observer. Definition or review alone
does not open an experiment grant.

## Authority belongs to a bounded task

One returned scope approval binds the exact target, admitted native baseline N,
exact Android recovery A, allowed operations and runtime profiles, permitted
functional source changes, finite time and operation budgets, and attended or
deferred physical recovery. It does not enumerate every future E image.
An in-scope spontaneous suggestion may become a new candidate without another
human approval. Each candidate still has its own machine-verified manifest,
boot-only AP, native input identity, qualification and immutable operation.
The agent may not enlarge or renew the approved scope or its budgets.

The scope may include fixed observation, N -> E -> N experiments, restoration
of admitted N from healthy Android, and a deliberate Android exit. Each actual
mode transition and transfer keeps its existing intent and result. There is
no generic command, arbitrary root shell, non-boot partition, new transport,
new physical target or implicit hardware-control capability.

The parent fixes policy and scope. Each child fixes the current independently
reviewed machinery and its exact recovery closure. A qualifying machinery
revision within the same accepted capability may be used by a later child
without another candidate approval; a running or stopped child retains its
original bindings. Changes to the accepted scope, policy, target or recovery
identity require a new scope. Actual attendance is checked when the selected
operation or recovery needs it.

The former one-E/600-second and three-reservation limits do not apply to this
explicit new mode. The operator supplies positive finite task budgets. Existing
per-session protocol, output, transfer and clock limits still apply; a longer
task is not a longer unauthenticated handshake or an unbounded device command.
The parent deadline never renews between children or after host suspend.

An interrupted unexecuted child selection may be completed from its unchanged
request, or cancelled after proving that it has no operation/effect records
and owns no F1 action. Cancellation retains its unique ordinal and all bytes;
only demonstrably unused operation capacity is returned. The parent deadline
and actual-operation budget do not grow. A child with a device reservation or
operation cannot use this H0 cancellation route.

## Observations and failures

A fixed read-only reobservation is authorized by the current foreground S22+
research task or a matching active scope. It needs no per-read approval,
candidate claim or new independent review while the observer capability and
its hazard-relevant inputs are unchanged. One invocation has a finite time
bound and sends only its declared handshake, fixed health/status/HUD reads and
clean DETACH. It cannot send CONTROL, reboot, start a service, change hardware
configuration or transfer an image.

An unexplained observation failure ends that attempt. Its raw evidence, any
consumed authentication ordinal and its failure record remain intact. A fresh
bounded observation may follow through the reviewed reobservation path, using
the exact target, a fresh nonce, checked boot/session state and an exclusive
transport. A transport error is not permission to widen the selector or send
a state-changing recovery command. Repeated observation needs an actual
diagnostic purpose and stops when its task budget is spent or it yields no
useful new evidence; there is no automatic polling loop.

The legacy P387 pure-D0 OPEN/RX-zero stop may be imported only through the
observer's exact retained-evidence reader. This creates a new observation
attempt referencing the old stop; it never reruns the old caller, edits its
records or reopens its closed F1 grant. A successful new observation may supply
a new, independently validated native-state tail. The previous failure remains
NO_PROOF. An observation tail cannot release an unresolved F1 recovery owner
or repeat an uncertain transfer or CONTROL.

Failure scope follows the possible effect. A scientific negative with proved
fixed health may complete the normal N return and the task may continue.
Malformed health evidence or uncertain state-changing execution blocks new
state-changing work on that target until the existing recovery and health
conditions close. Independent host work and otherwise permitted observations
may continue. A test result, normal return, terminal health and causal
explanation remain separate claims.

## Prospective repeat experiments

Historical consumed candidates and installation claims are unchanged and
ineligible for this new repeat class. An E first installed by this new mode
becomes repeat-eligible only after its exact original global claim, qualified
E health and a complete healthy `NATIVE_CLOSED` return to N are rederived. Its scientific result need
not be positive. A later repeat is a new scoped operation, with a new role
intent, exact current artifacts, fresh target health and no unresolved owner.
The original global claim stays active; no release, overwritten intent or
renamed old run manufactures another attempt.

An uncertain operation never resumes its E/N/CONTROL effects. Its predefined
recovery may close from the journal; only a later independently intended
operation with proved closure and current health may proceed. A first E that
never qualified for the repeat class cannot be adopted through this rule.

## Qualification and source identity

Capability review binds target selection, authentication, command boundaries,
transfer/archive handling, recovery, ownership, raw evidence and health/terminal
readers. Candidate registration is declarative data selected through that
reviewed machinery. Adding a catalog row or changing a run ID, version or
candidate AP does not by itself invalidate its capability review.

The request names permitted functional source changes inside the reviewed
profile. Each fresh candidate binds and validates its actual build inputs;
changing hardware access, privilege, transport, recovery or health semantics
requires review of that changed hazard-relevant scope. Path membership is not
proof that arbitrary code has no side effects. Relevant real producer/consumer
tests and target-ABI checks remain required when those behaviors change.
Unchanged checks and qualified build outputs are reused.

The capability receipt retains the independently reviewed functional reference
hashes. Preparing a new scope does not silently qualify edited collector or
renderer code. Changed executable fragment bytes require a focused independent
source-effect assessment bound to those bytes, their nonmutable native context,
runtime profile and capability constraints. That assessment can be reused for
other candidates with the same covered inputs; candidate IDs, versions and AP
data do not require another assessment or human approval. This avoids treating
an allowed filename as proof that new hardware/privilege effects are absent.

Admitted N and repeat-qualified E retain their verified byte identity and
original source provenance. Later edits to a development source file do not
change the already-qualified image. Their artifacts and retained proof must
still rederive; current host readers and the selected wire profile must remain
compatible. This is distinct from qualifying a freshly built E from current
sources. Unrelated goals, reports, other-target records and catalog data are
not execution authority. Shared safety-policy changes still require review.

## Deferred physical recovery

An explicitly selected deferred scope accepts a powered, potentially active
N/E/Download state while physical recovery waits for the operator. Normal
machine-controlled operations need no attendance within that scope. This does
not claim automatic kernel/USB-stall recovery, quiescence, bounded battery
consumption or guaranteed notification after host failure. A known new hazard
requiring prompt physical intervention excludes unattended execution.

On an uncertain state-changing failure, retain the F1 owner and the original
one-shot A; close that child's research effects. Later A requires actual
attendance, exact Download binding and the original recovery closure. Expiry
does not revoke that recovery or permit its replay. No next experiment starts
over an unhealthy or unverified device. Safe read-only diagnosis does not
itself establish safe recovery or complete the F1 owner.

## Explicit interaction with existing policies

The common boundary-7 observation delegation, task-scoped D1/F1 approval and
prospective-repeat exception apply only through this new mode and observer.
They specialize the legacy per-action approval, fixed E/catalog, clean-session
continuation and one-shot content restrictions only as stated above. Absent
mode V1/V2, the earlier deferred-physical mode, ordinary Process v2, existing
approvals and their historical readers keep their existing meanings. Other
targets receive no authority from this adoption.

Routine runs use the existing structured results, journals and private raw
evidence. A new capability, changed hazard or incident merits one concise
report; ordinary reads and candidates do not get new policy documents or
approval ceremonies. This capability and its permanent boundaries require one
independent review of the reachable implementation and relevant failure paths.

## Entry points

The scope owner provides `prepare`, `grant`, `select`, `execute`, `status`,
`close`, `complete-selection` and `cancel-child`. `prepare` returns the one
scope approval for the operator; `select` binds a manifest or retained repeat
admission to that original grant and returns the child grant for `execute`.
`--source-assessment` supplies a reusable reviewed executable-change receipt
when needed. `status` reads records only. `close` ends future task authority;
it does not retire an unresolved child's recovery owner.

The fixed observer provides `observe --previous ... --output ... --purpose ...`
and an H0 `read --terminal ...`. Previous records are exact private terminal or
supported stopped-attempt paths. Outputs must be new private run directories.
Neither entry point synthesizes a parent approval or automatically repeats an
observation. The shared baseline owner's existing attended recovery command
remains the entry point for an unresolved child's original A.
